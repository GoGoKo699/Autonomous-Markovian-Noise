# A finite autonomous bath

One time-independent finite Hamiltonian can approximate the complete dephasing process while controlling its memory, interaction strength, and bath spectral width simultaneously. This page constructs that Hamiltonian and proves its error bound. The operational distance and admissible testers are defined in [the model](model.md); the combined statement appears in [the resource theorem](resource_theorem.md).

The target generator is $`\mathcal L_\gamma(\rho)=\gamma(Z\rho Z-\rho)`$. We retain a general horizon $`T`$ in the estimates and specialize to $`\gamma T=1`$ for the resource scaling. For integers $`m\ge2`$ and $`k\ge1`$, the construction has

```math
D=\binom{4m-3+k}{k},\qquad
\Lambda\le\sqrt{\frac{3k\gamma m}{2T}},\qquad
W_E=\frac{2\pi k(m-1)}{T},
```

and its error against every allowed tester with at most $`N`$ contacts obeys

```math
d_N\le (2N+1)\frac{3\gamma T}{m}+E_k(\gamma T),
\qquad
E_k(J):=\sum_{n=k+1}^{\infty}
\frac{\sqrt{(2n-1)!!}}{n!}J^{n/2}.
\tag{1}
```

The first term is the [adaptive Gaussian comparison](adaptive_comparison.md). The second is a bound on the dynamics generated by a Hamiltonian compressed to total occupation at most $`k`$. It includes paths that leave that subspace and subsequently return. A bound on final occupation probability would not by itself establish (1).

## 1. A finite positive covariance

Set $`L=4T`$. Define

```math
J_m(\theta)=
\frac{\left|\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}e^{ij\theta}\right|^4}{c_0},
\qquad
c_0=\frac{2m^3+m}{3},
\qquad
K_m(t)=\frac{\gamma}{L}J_m(2\pi t/L).
\tag{2}
```

This squared Fejer, or Jackson-type, kernel is pointwise nonnegative. Its Fourier coefficients are also nonnegative. Indeed, if $`q_j=(m-|j|)_+`$, then

```math
\left|\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}e^{ij\theta}\right|^2
=\sum_jq_je^{ij\theta},
\qquad
c_r:=\sum_jq_jq_{r-j}\ge0.
```

The coefficients are symmetric and have support $`|r|\le2m-2`$. Their constant coefficient is

```math
c_0=m^2+2\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}j^2=\frac{2m^3+m}{3}.
```

Consequently

```math
K_m(t)=\sum_{r=-(2m-2)}^{2m-2}a_re^{-i\omega_rt},
\qquad
a_r=\frac{\gamma c_r}{Lc_0},
\qquad
\omega_r=\frac{2\pi r}{L}.
\tag{3}
```

There are $`M=4m-3`$ modes. The kernel is real, even, positive definite, nonnegative at every time, and has integral $`\gamma`$ over one period. No approximation of a Fourier integral is used.

### First moment and integrated covariance

Let

```math
\mu_m=\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}|t|K_m(t)\,dt,
\qquad
J=\int_0^Tdt\int_0^Tds\,K_m(t-s).
```

For $`|\theta|\le\pi`$,

```math
\left|\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}e^{ij\theta}\right|
\le\min\{m,\pi/|\theta|\},
\qquad
c_0\ge\frac{2m^3}{3}.
```

Changing variables in the first moment and splitting at $`\theta=\pi/m`$ gives

```math
\begin{aligned}
\mu_m
&=\frac{\gamma L}{2\pi^2}\int_0^\pi\theta J_m(\theta)\,d\theta\\
&\le\frac{3\gamma L}{4\pi^2}
\left[
m\int_0^{\pi/m}\theta\,d\theta
+\frac{\pi^4}{m^3}\int_{\pi/m}^{\pi}\theta^{-3}\,d\theta
\right]\\
&=\frac{3\gamma L}{4m}\left(1-\frac{1}{2m^2}\right)
\le\frac{3\gamma T}{m}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
```

The exact peak and largest frequency are

```math
K_m(0)=\frac{3\gamma m^3}{L(2m^2+1)}
\le\frac{3\gamma m}{2L},
\qquad
\omega_{\max}=\frac{4\pi(m-1)}L=\frac{\pi(m-1)}T.
\tag{5}
```

Each row of the square integral defining $`J`$ integrates the nonnegative periodic kernel over an interval shorter than one period. Hence

```math
J\le\gamma T.
\tag{6}
```

Both types of positivity have distinct roles. Fourier positivity supplies a Gaussian covariance. Pointwise positivity controls the contact comparison and the deleted-path expansion below.

## 2. The Gaussian oscillator reference

On the bosonic Fock space of these finitely many modes, introduce

```math
H_E^{\infty}=\sum_r\omega_rb_r^\dagger b_r,
\qquad
B^{\infty}=\sum_r g_r(b_r+b_r^\dagger),
\qquad
g_r=\sqrt{a_r}.
\tag{7}
```

The reference bath starts in the vacuum $`|0\rangle`$. This infinite-dimensional reference is a proof device; it is not the final simulator.

### A well-defined unitary with signed frequencies

The signed frequencies in (7) do not obstruct self-adjointness. Let $`\mathcal N`$ be total occupation, $`P_r`$ the projector onto occupation at most $`r`$, and $`\Omega=\max_j|\omega_j|`$. On finite-particle vectors, the symmetric operator

```math
H^{\infty}=I\otimes H_E^{\infty}+Z\otimes B^{\infty}
```

maps $`P_r`$ into $`P_{r+1}`$ and satisfies

```math
\|H^{\infty}P_r\|
\le\Omega r+\|g\|(\sqrt r+\sqrt{r+1})
\le C(r+1),\qquad C:=\Omega+2\|g\|.
```

Therefore, for $`\psi=P_r\psi`$,

```math
\|(H^{\infty})^p\psi\|
\le C^p\frac{(r+p)!}{r!}\|\psi\|,
\qquad
\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\frac{|t|^p}{p!}
\|(H^{\infty})^p\psi\|
\le\frac{\|\psi\|}{(1-C|t|)^{r+1}}
\quad(C|t|\lt1).
```

The finite-particle domain is dense and consists of analytic vectors. The analytic-vector criterion therefore makes this symmetric operator essentially self-adjoint; its closure generates a unique unitary group. This is the standard criterion of [Nelson](https://doi.org/10.2307/1970331), with its hypotheses verified above. The argument uses $`|\omega_j|`$ and includes the zero-frequency mode.

There is also an explicit conditional-oscillator description. For $`Z=\sigma\in\{-1,1\}`$ and $`\omega_r\ne0`$, the mode Hamiltonian is a displaced signed number operator:

```math
\omega_rb_r^\dagger b_r+\sigma g_r(b_r+b_r^\dagger)
=D(\sigma g_r/\omega_r)^\dagger
\omega_rb_r^\dagger b_rD(\sigma g_r/\omega_r)
-\frac{g_r^2}{\omega_r},
```

where $`D(\alpha)^\dagger bD(\alpha)=b+\alpha`$. The zero-frequency term is a self-adjoint coordinate operator. Thus neither a positive-frequency assumption nor a formal use of a divergent perturbation series is needed to define the reference evolution.

### Commuting fields and the complete Gaussian process

In the free-bath interaction picture,

```math
B(t)=\sum_rg_r\left(b_re^{-i\omega_rt}+b_r^\dagger e^{i\omega_rt}\right),
\qquad
\langle0|B(t)B(s)|0\rangle=K_m(t-s).
\tag{8}
```

The fields strongly commute. To see this explicitly, write the canonical coordinates of a positive/negative frequency pair as $`q_\pm,p_\pm`$. The operators

```math
X_r=q_r+q_{-r},\qquad Y_r=p_r-p_{-r}
```

commute and, after a change of mode coordinates, are a position coordinate of one mode and a momentum coordinate of another. Different pairs commute as well. Together with $`X_0=b_0+b_0^\dagger`$, these are independent standard real Gaussian variables in the vacuum spectral measure. In these variables,

```math
B(t)=g_0X_0+
\sqrt2\sum_{r=1}^{2m-2}g_r
\bigl[X_r\cos(\omega_rt)+Y_r\sin(\omega_rt)\bigr].
\tag{9}
```

Let $`\Phi_t=\int_0^tB(s)\,ds`$, where the integral denotes the corresponding finite linear combination of canonical fields. Strong commutativity and free-bath covariance show that

```math
e^{-itH^{\infty}}
=e^{-it(I\otimes H_E^{\infty})}
\exp[-iZ\otimes\Phi_t].
```

Indeed, the right side is a strongly continuous unitary group: the field integrals concatenate after free-bath conjugation. Its generator agrees with the formal Hamiltonian on the finite-particle domain, so essential self-adjointness identifies it with the unitary already defined above. This supplies the interaction-picture factorization without assuming an unproved rule for unbounded time-ordered exponentials.

Equation (9) identifies the reference with a real Gaussian field of covariance $`K_m`$. Since every field lies in the same commuting observable algebra, this identity holds for the complete controlled process, including noncommuting system instruments, retained references, outcomes, and feedback. It is stronger than an equality of uncontrolled one-time channels.

## 3. Controls and the meaning of the Dyson expansion

First fix a finite classical timing tree. Dilate its complete instruments to unitaries on the system and an enlarged tester memory, keeping copies of the classical records. All tester ancillas may be supplied at time zero. Conditional operations are then fixed record-controlled unitaries at the finite union of possible contact times. These ancillary systems belong to the tester, not to the device bath.

In the interaction frame of these controls and the free bath, the coupling is

```math
A(t)\otimes B(t),\qquad
A(t)=V_c(t)^\dagger(Z\otimes I)V_c(t),\qquad
\|A(t)\|=1.
\tag{10}
```

The operator $`A(t)`$ is a fixed tester operator, not a bath-dependent random operator. It need not commute with $`A(s)`$. Memory-only processing commutes with $`Z`$ and creates no additional difficulty.

The spectral representation (9) also gives a direct justification of the controlled Dyson series. For each real Gaussian coordinate vector $`x`$, the field is a scalar continuous function $`\xi(t,x)`$, and the conditional evolution solves the bounded-operator equation

```math
\frac{d}{dt}V_x(t)=-iA(t)\xi(t,x)V_x(t),\qquad V_x(0)=I.
```

For fixed finitely many modes, $`\int_0^T|\xi(t,x)|dt\le c_{m,T}\|x\|`$. Its ordinary Dyson series is bounded termwise by the exponential of this quantity. That exponential is square integrable in the finite-dimensional Gaussian measure. The series therefore converges on the vacuum input in Hilbert-space norm and equals the actual controlled reference unitary. This existence argument may use a bandwidth-dependent dominating function; the uniform error estimate below does not.

## 4. Compressing the Hamiltonian to a fixed occupation sector

Let $`P_k`$ retain total occupation at most $`k`$. The actual finite device is

```math
H^{(k)}=I\otimes H_E^{(k)}+Z\otimes B^{(k)},
\qquad
H_E^{(k)}=P_kH_E^{\infty}P_k,
\qquad
B^{(k)}=P_kB^{\infty}P_k,
\tag{11}
```

restricted to $`P_k\mathcal F`$, initially in $`|0\rangle`$. This is an ordinary finite Hermitian matrix. There is no projection, measurement, or conditioning during its operation. Since $`[P_k,H_E^{\infty}]=0`$, its interaction-picture field is exactly $`P_kB(t)P_k`$.

### Deleted ladder paths have positive overlaps

For ordered times $`0\lt t_1\lt\cdots\lt t_n\lt T`$, define

```math
\begin{aligned}
v(t)&=B(t_n)\cdots B(t_1)|0\rangle,\\
v_k(t)&=P_kB(t_n)P_k\cdots P_kB(t_1)P_k|0\rangle,\\
w_k(t)&=v(t)-v_k(t).
\end{aligned}
```

Expand each field into its creation and annihilation parts. A ladder pattern has a definite particle number after every prefix. Inserting $`P_k`$ deletes precisely the patterns whose prefix occupation ever exceeds $`k`$ and leaves every other coefficient unchanged. This criterion is independent of mode labels. Consequently $`w_k`$ is the sum of the deleted patterns and vanishes for $`n\le k`$.

For two time tuples $`s,t`$, expand $`\langle w_k(s),w_k(t)\rangle`$. The vacuum expectation of any pair of selected ladder words is a sum of Wick contractions. Every allowed contraction equals a value of $`K_m`$, which is real and nonnegative. A complete pairing fixes which positions are creation and annihilation operators, so the deleted-word restriction selects a subset of the full Wick sum, without changing its coefficients. Pointwise,

```math
0\le\langle w_k(s),w_k(t)\rangle
\le\langle v(s),v(t)\rangle
=\langle0|B(s_1)\cdots B(s_n)B(t_n)\cdots B(t_1)|0\rangle.
\tag{12}
```

This is a scalar vacuum-overlap inequality. It is not an operator-monotonicity assertion about projecting Hamiltonians.

### The excursion error is independent of bandwidth

Let $`W_n`$ be the difference of the full and compressed $`n`$th Dyson contributions applied to an arbitrary normalized pure tester input and the vacuum. The accompanying tester word $`A(t_n)\cdots A(t_1)`$ has norm at most one. Taking the absolute value of the tester overlap and applying (12) yields

```math
\|W_n\|^2
\le\int_{\Delta_n}ds\int_{\Delta_n}dt\,
\langle v(s),v(t)\rangle,
\qquad
\Delta_n=\{0\lt t_1\lt\cdots\lt t_n\lt T\}.
```

The full scalar moment is symmetric in all $`2n`$ time variables. There are $`(2n-1)!!`$ pairings. Extending each ordered simplex to its cube contributes $`1/n!`$, and every pairing integrates to $`J^n`$. Thus

```math
W_n=0\quad(n\le k),\qquad
\|W_n\|\le
a_n(J):=\frac{\sqrt{(2n-1)!!}}{n!}J^{n/2}\quad(n\gt k).
\tag{13}
```

The same majorant controls the full Dyson term. It is summable because

```math
\frac{a_{n+1}(J)}{a_n(J)}
=\frac{\sqrt{(2n+1)J}}{n+1}\longrightarrow0.
```

It follows that the joint state-vector discrepancy is at most $`E_k(J)`$. Returning from the interaction frames preserves this distance: the same tester unitary acts in both constructions, and the compressed free bath is the restriction of the full free bath. Half trace distance between the resulting pure states is no greater than their vector distance. Partial trace then gives, for every finite tester tree,

```math
\frac12\|\rho_{\rm reference}-\rho_{\rm finite}\|_1
\le E_k(J)\le E_k(\gamma T).
\tag{14}
```

This controls all deleted excursions, including those that return to low occupation before the experiment ends. Its essential hypotheses are a vacuum input, total-number compression, a mode-independent ladder-path criterion, and pointwise nonnegative real covariance. Positive definiteness alone would not justify (12).

## 5. Explicit choices and finite resources

For computation, the decreasing ratio in (13) gives the certificate

```math
E_k(J)\le
\frac{a_{k+1}(J)}{1-r_k(J)},\qquad
r_k(J):=\frac{\sqrt{(2k+3)J}}{k+2}\lt1.
\tag{15}
```

At $`J=1`$, choose any $`k=k_\epsilon`$ for which the right side of (15) is at most $`\epsilon/2`$, and choose

```math
m=\max\left\{2,\left\lceil\frac{6(2N+1)}\epsilon\right\rceil\right\}.
\tag{16}
```

For example, (15) certifies $`k=10`$ at $`\epsilon=10^{-2}`$, $`k=13`$ at $`10^{-3}`$, and $`k=19`$ at $`10^{-6}`$. No cutoff depending on $`N`$ is required at fixed accuracy. A simple closed-form sufficient choice is $`k\ge\max\{21,\lceil\log_2(2/\epsilon)\rceil\}`$: the inequality $`a_n(1)\le(2e/n)^{n/2}`$ implies $`E_k(1)\le2^{-k}`$ whenever $`k+1\ge8e`$.

There are $`\binom{M+k}{k}`$ occupation strings of weight at most $`k`$. The compressed annihilation operator has norm $`\sqrt{k}\|g\|`$, and its compressed adjoint has the same norm. Equations (5) and (11) therefore give

```math
\begin{aligned}
D&=\binom{4m-3+k}{k},\\
\Lambda=\|B^{(k)}\|
&\le2\sqrt{k}\|g\|
=2\sqrt{kK_m(0)}
\le\sqrt{\frac{3k\gamma m}{2T}},\\
W_E&=2k\omega_{\max}
=\frac{2\pi k(m-1)}T.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
```

The width equality holds because both extreme modes exist and all $`k`$ particles may occupy either of them. At $`\gamma T=1`$, (16)-(17) yield

```math
\log_2D=O_\epsilon(\log(N+2)),\qquad
\Lambda=O_\epsilon(\gamma\sqrt{N+1}),\qquad
W_E=O_\epsilon(\gamma(N+1)).
```

To conclude the operational statement, first combine (14) with the finite-tree bound in [adaptive comparison](adaptive_comparison.md). This gives (1) directly between the actual finite device and the Markov target for every finite timing tree. Pass that final inequality to the operational closure defined in [the model](model.md). No convergence of an intermediate infinite Gaussian reference is assumed in defining that closure.

## 6. Physical scope and attribution

The finite bath has one autonomous Hamiltonian and a pure initial state. All its modes and retained occupation states are present at time zero. There is no external switching, arriving ancilla, discarded record, or reset.

The positive and negative frequencies are part of the construction. After compression, adding $`k\omega_{\max}I`$ makes the free-bath spectrum nonnegative without changing its width or dynamics. The vacuum then has free-bath energy $`k\omega_{\max}`$, in the middle of that spectrum. It is an excited reference state, not a ground or passive state. The Hamiltonian may be global in an encoding into qubits; geometric locality and efficient synthesis are not established.

Gaussian representations, Jackson kernels, Wick expansions, and occupation truncation are established tools. Trivedi, Malz and Cirac provide both factorial particle-sector bounds and a comparison with projected-Hamiltonian dynamics using a boundary-coupling integral. The specific estimate needed here controls all omitted excursions uniformly as the bandwidth grows, using the integrated covariance instead of the increasing equal-time field variance. It is combined with the adaptive-process estimate to obtain simultaneous finite resources. Detailed comparisons and primary references are collected in [related work](../references/RELATED_WORK.md).

The lower bounds and their inherited ingredients are given in [dynamical bounds](dynamical_bounds.md) and [the memory bound](memory_bound.md). The linear-width conclusion is conditional on keeping coupling at its optimal square-root order; it is not a separate unrestricted bandwidth lower bound.
