# Model and operational error

[Project overview](../README.md) · [Resource theorem](resource_theorem.md)

## The question

A Markovian channel can be applied after each intervention without retaining a bath history. A finite closed environment instead evolves jointly with the system and carries the information created by earlier interactions. Matching a single terminal channel does not ensure that these two descriptions remain close when the system is interrogated repeatedly.

We ask for one autonomous finite environment whose entire controlled process approximates qubit dephasing, uniformly over a prescribed contact budget. The environment is designed for the rate, horizon, accuracy and contact budget, but not for the initial system state, intervention times, instruments or observed outcomes.

## Target and simulator

Let $`S=\mathbb C^2`$ and let $`X,Y,Z`$ denote Pauli matrices. Fix $`\gamma\gt0`$. The target generator is

```math
\mathcal L_\gamma(\rho)=\gamma(Z\rho Z-\rho).
```

An off-diagonal matrix entry decays by $`e^{-2\gamma t}`$ in an uninterrupted interval of duration $`t`$. Equivalently, the interval channel is

```math
\mathcal E_t(\rho)=(1-p_t)\rho+p_tZ\rho Z,
\qquad p_t=\frac{1-e^{-2\gamma t}}{2}.
```

Between successive contacts the target applies this semigroup to $`S`$, leaving the tester's memory untouched. The main theorem fixes $`T=1/\gamma`$.

A microscopic device consists of a finite Hilbert space $`E`$ of dimension $`D`$, an initial bath state $`\omega_E`$, and

```math
H=I_S\otimes H_E+Z\otimes B,
\qquad H_E=H_E^\dagger,\quad B=B^\dagger.
```

The initial state is $`\rho_{SR}\otimes\omega_E`$, where $`R`$ is controlled by the tester. The bath state and Hamiltonian are fixed before the tester operates. Evolution between contacts is generated by the same $`H`$ throughout $`[0,T]`$. There is no bath reset, discarded bath subsystem, incoming ancilla or externally supplied switching schedule. Any auxiliary degree of freedom belonging to the device must be included in $`E`$.

The upper construction uses a pure $`\omega_E`$ satisfying $`[\omega_E,H_E]=0`$. The lower bounds allow an arbitrary mixed bath state. There is no assumption that $`\omega_E`$ is thermal, a ground state or passive.

## Testers and the contact budget

A finite tester has a protected finite-dimensional quantum memory of arbitrary size, any initial state on $`S R`$, and a finite classical decision tree. Contact times are arbitrary real times strictly inside $`(0,T)`$, ordered along each history; no uniform grid or minimum separation is imposed. At a contact, the tester applies an arbitrary finite-outcome quantum instrument to $`S R`$. Its subsequent instrument and time choices can depend on earlier classical outcomes.

The contact count is a structural property of the policy: every leaf contains at most $`N`$ declared contacts, including branches that happen to have zero probability for a particular model. A declared system–memory instrument counts as a contact even if one of its outcomes leaves the system unchanged. Outcomes cannot be discarded by conditioning on success when defining the comparison.

Operations acting only on $`R`$ and classical records are uncharged. The tester may prepare its initial state and perform an arbitrary final readout of its accessible registers. The final readout defines an event and is not an additional interior intervention. All intermediate system access is counted. Coincident contacts can be combined into one instrument without increasing the budget.

Timing is controlled by classical records. A coherent superposition of contact times and uncounted continuous access to $`S`$ are outside this class. Purifying an instrument or retaining an analytical copy of a record in a proof does not give the operational tester extra access.

## Distance and operational closure

For a finite tester $`\mathsf T`$, let $`\rho^{\mathsf T}_{\rm dev}`$ and $`\rho^{\mathsf T}_{\rm M}`$ denote its final accessible output states, including retained classical outcomes. Define

```math
d_N^{\rm fin}({\rm dev},{\rm M})
=\sup_{\mathsf T}\frac12
\left\|\rho^{\mathsf T}_{\rm dev}-\rho^{\mathsf T}_{\rm M}\right\|_1.
```

For each finite output, half trace distance equals the largest difference in probability of a final binary event. The supremum includes arbitrary protected memory and final measurements. Independent classical mixtures of finite testers are admitted; convexity preserves every uniform bound used below.

We also admit their **operational closure**. A limiting tester is admissible when, under a common identification of its declared final events, a sequence of finite admissible testers with the same structural contact budget has convergent event probabilities for the target and for every finite microscopic device. This is pointwise operational convergence. No uniform convergence over all devices, no uniform bound on memory dimension, and no convergence to an auxiliary infinite bath are assumed.

For the complete class, including these limits, the operational error is

```math
d_N({\rm dev},{\rm M})
=\sup_{\mathsf T,\,A}
\left|p_{\rm dev}^{\mathsf T}(A)-p_{\rm M}^{\mathsf T}(A)\right|,
```

where $`A`$ is an allowed final event. This agrees with the half trace-distance formulation on finite testers without presuming a common quantum-output state space for every operational limit.

The proof first bounds the error between the target and the actual constructed finite device on finite trees. That final event inequality then passes to the closure. It is unnecessary to extend the intermediate Gaussian comparison separately to every limiting tester. This is the operational timing convention used in the [intervention-resolution account](https://github.com/GoGoKo699/Markovian-Resolution-Limit/blob/b4af5cfb7a86165f29d9cf1e9adcb74f5fe91a8b/science/model.md).

## Charged resources

The resources of the same device are

```math
Q=\left\lceil\log_2 D\right\rceil,\qquad
\Lambda=\|B\|,\qquad
W_E=\lambda_{\max}(H_E)-\lambda_{\min}(H_E).
```

The ceiling in $`Q`$ changes asymptotic statements by at most one qubit. A scalar shift of $`H_E`$ has no effect on the process. In particular,

```math
\min_{c\in\mathbb R}\|H_E-cI\|=\frac{W_E}{2}.
```

The memory resource is encoded Hilbert-space size. It does not charge the number of native oscillator modes separately, assert a local encoding, or bound the circuit required to synthesize the Hamiltonian. The width measures the free bath spectrum, rather than the full interacting spectrum or thermodynamic work. These distinctions are used explicitly in the [interpretation](interpretation.md).
